Figure S1. Principal-component projection of the 546-record growing-pig target domain and the 80-record gestating-sow target domain onto the predictor space defined by the historical data. Historical records are shown as the gray reference cloud. This historical-data-defined projection is distinct from the target-diet PCA used for measurement selection in Figure 4; the PC1/PC2 variance percentages therefore need not be identical between the two figures. PC, principal component.

Figure S2. Predictor-distribution differences relative to the 673-record development set. (A) Signed standardized mean difference; (B) normalized Wasserstein distance; and (C) percentage of observations outside the development-set range. FN and UN outcomes are excluded from these predictor-distribution comparisons. Each panel uses its own color scale appropriate to the displayed metric; color intensity should not be compared directly across panels. FN, fecal nitrogen; UN, urinary nitrogen.

Figure S3. Calibration and agreement of the historical model in independent evaluation datasets before target-domain model updating. From top to bottom, rows represent the 233-record confirmation set for FN and UN, the 546-record target system for FN and UN, and the gestating-sow evaluation before updating for FN and UN. Points show calibration slope (A), calibration intercept (B), and concordance correlation coefficient (C); horizontal bars show the corresponding 95% intervals.

Figure S4. Pre-update prediction-error heterogeneity across ingredient classes in the 546-record target dataset. Panels A and B show ingredient-class RMSE for fecal nitrogen (FN) and urinary nitrogen (UN), respectively, using the historical model before target-domain FN/UN measurements were acquired. All 91 source-specific diets contribute to the subgroup summaries (10 diets per class except paddy rice, n = 11). RMSE, root mean squared error.

Figure S5. Locations of selected source-specific diets in target predictor space across measurement budgets of 3, 6, 9, and 12 diets. Columns show P2-PAM representative sampling using k-medoids, response-surface-guided sampling, and random sampling. For random sampling, marker size is proportional to selection frequency across 504 balanced outcome-record allocation scenarios; the size legend provides reference frequencies of 5%, 10%, and 15%. PAM, partitioning around medoids.

Figure S6. Performance across measurement-selection strategies with CG-HPP updating fixed. Mean NRMSE (A), FN R² (B), and UN R² (C) are shown at independently defined total measurement budgets of 3, 6, 9, and 12 source-specific diets. P2-PAM representative sampling and response-surface-guided source sets are optimized separately at each budget and are not constrained to be nested; random sampling uses nested prefixes of a random source permutation within each realization. Curves show means across 504 balanced outcome-record allocations; descriptive percentile ranges are not displayed. CG-HPP, conditional Gaussian hierarchical partial pooling; FN, fecal nitrogen; NRMSE, normalized root mean squared error; PAM, partitioning around medoids; UN, urinary nitrogen.

Figure S7. Performance across model-updating methods and target-only comparator models with response-surface-guided sampling fixed. Mean NRMSE (A), FN R² (B), and UN R² (C) are shown across 3, 6, 9, and 12 measured source-specific diets. Curves show means across 504 balanced outcome-record allocations; descriptive percentile ranges are not displayed. Dashed curves denote target-only models, whereas solid curves denote the historical no-update control or update procedures that retain the historical model. CG-HPP, conditional Gaussian hierarchical partial pooling; FN, fecal nitrogen; NRMSE, normalized root mean squared error; UN, urinary nitrogen.

Figure S8. Within-diet measurement-depth analysis. Panels show mean NRMSE (A), FN R² (B), and UN R² (C) across measurement budgets of 3, 6, 9, and 12 source-specific diets when 1, 3, or 6 pigs were measured per selected diet. Lines show means; shaded bands are descriptive 2.5th–97.5th percentiles across balanced within-diet outcome-record allocations for 1 and 3 pigs per diet. No band is shown for 6 pigs because all six observed animals are used. FN, fecal nitrogen; NRMSE, normalized root mean squared error; UN, urinary nitrogen.

Figure S9. Model-specific performance–budget curves with CG-HPP updating fixed. Panels A–C show mean NRMSE, FN R², and UN R², respectively, when MLR, RF, TabM, and MLR+RF each use predictions from their own fixed historical model to guide response-surface selection. Source sets are independently optimized at each total budget and are not nested. The same 504 balanced outcome-record allocation scenarios are used for every model type. Model types are distinguished by both color and point shape. The gray region begins after the 12-diet budget and denotes the post hoc extension to budgets of 15, 18, and 21 diets. CG-HPP, conditional Gaussian hierarchical partial pooling; FN, fecal nitrogen; MLR, multiple linear regression; NRMSE, normalized root mean squared error; RF, random forest; TabM, tabular neural network; UN, urinary nitrogen.

Figure S10. Updating-method performance–budget curves under model-specific response-surface-guided source selection. Panels A–D show mean NRMSE for MLR, RF, TabM, and MLR+RF, respectively, across eight update/control methods and budgets of 3–21 source-specific diets. Source sets are independently optimized at each total budget and are not nested. Dashed curves denote target-only models. All curves are means across the same 504 balanced outcome-record allocation scenarios; measured source-specific diets are excluded from evaluation. The gray region begins after the 12-diet budget and denotes the post hoc extension to budgets of 15, 18, and 21 diets. CG-HPP, conditional Gaussian hierarchical partial pooling; MLR, multiple linear regression; NRMSE, normalized root mean squared error; RF, random forest; TabM, tabular neural network.
