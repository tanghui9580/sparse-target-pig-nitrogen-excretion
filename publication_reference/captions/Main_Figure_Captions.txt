Figure 1. Study design and analysis sequence. Predictor information and model structure were determined in 673 development records from 122 study groups and then evaluated once in 233 records from 48 previously unseen studies. The prespecified model was refitted to all 906 historical records and transferred without updating to an independent target system containing 91 source-specific diets and 546 pig-period records. Target-measurement allocation and model updating were compared at prespecified budgets of 3, 6, 9, and 12 diets; each selected diet contributed one pig-period FN/UN record. Budgets of 15, 18, and 21 diets were post hoc extensions. The measurement-selection comparison included random sampling, P2-PAM representative sampling, and response-surface-guided sampling. FN and UN were modeled directly, whereas RN and TNE were derived by nitrogen mass balance. The gestating-sow dataset was used for cross-physiology evaluation. CG-HPP, conditional Gaussian hierarchical partial pooling; FN, fecal nitrogen; P0–P3, prespecified predictor-information tiers; PAM, partitioning around medoids; RN, retained nitrogen; TNE, total nitrogen excretion; UN, urinary nitrogen.

Figure 2. Model development, held-out study evaluation, and direct transfer to the target system. (A) The 10 candidate models in the 673-record development set, showing mean NRMSE together with endpoint-specific FN and UN performance. Circles, squares, triangles, and diamonds denote linear models, tree ensembles, the tabular neural network, and linear–nonlinear hybrid models, respectively; color distinguishes the development-selected MLR+RF model, the retained MLR/RF/TabM comparators, and the remaining candidates. (B) Mean NRMSE in the 233-record held-out historical dataset and the independent target system. (C) UN prediction in the target system before updating. Target-domain data were not used to repeat model selection. FN, fecal nitrogen; MLR, multiple linear regression; NRMSE, normalized root mean squared error; RF, random forest; RMSE, root mean squared error; TabM, tabular neural network; UN, urinary nitrogen.

Figure 3. FN and UN prediction across target-measurement budgets for response-surface-guided sampling with CG-HPP updating. (A) FN R²; (B) FN RMSE; (C) UN R²; and (D) UN RMSE. Filled points denote the prespecified budgets of 3, 6, 9, and 12 source-specific diets, whereas open points denote the post hoc extension budgets of 15, 18, and 21 diets. For CG-HPP, solid segments connect the prespecified budgets through 12 diets and dashed segments denote the post hoc extension beyond 12 diets. The no-update control is shown as a dashed blue line throughout; its marker fill follows the same prespecified-versus-post-hoc convention. The gray region begins after the 12-diet budget and denotes the post hoc extension. The response-surface-guided source set was independently optimized at each total budget and was not constrained to be nested. At each budget, the no-update control and CG-HPP updating were evaluated only on completely unmeasured source-specific diets (n = 88, 85, 82, 79, 76, 73, and 70, respectively). No-update performance can therefore vary across budgets because a different set of measured diets is removed from evaluation at each independently optimized budget. Points are means across 504 balanced outcome-record allocations; percentile ranges are not shown in this figure. CG-HPP, conditional Gaussian hierarchical partial pooling; FN, fecal nitrogen; RMSE, root mean squared error; UN, urinary nitrogen.

Figure 4. Target-measurement allocation and model updating. (A) Source-specific diets selected by random sampling, P2-PAM representative sampling using k-medoids, and response-surface-guided sampling at budgets of 3, 6, 9, and 12 diets. The two structured methods were optimized independently at each budget and were not constrained to be nested; random sets were nested prefixes of a random permutation within each allocation. (B) Mean NRMSE for the three allocation strategies with CG-HPP fixed; each strategy was evaluated on the diets left unmeasured by that strategy, with the paired comparison on diets left unmeasured by both reported in Supplementary Table S16. (C) Mean NRMSE for selected updating methods with response-surface-guided sampling fixed; the complete eight-method comparison is reported in Supplementary Figure S7 and the accompanying supplementary tables. Points are means across 504 balanced outcome-record allocations, and vertical ranges show descriptive 2.5th–97.5th percentiles; they are not confidence intervals. AUBC is the normalized area under the mean NRMSE–budget curve over 3, 6, 9, and 12 diets; lower values indicate lower integrated error. AUBC, area under the budget curve; CG-HPP, conditional Gaussian hierarchical partial pooling; NRMSE, normalized root mean squared error; PAM, partitioning around medoids.

Figure 5. Prediction recovery across starting models, physiology, and nitrogen endpoints. (A) AUBC in the growing-pig target system for MLR, RF, TabM, and MLR+RF; each starting model used its own fixed historical predictions to select response-surface-guided measurement sets before the same CG-HPP update. Horizontal ranges are the descriptive 2.5th–97.5th percentiles across 504 balanced outcome-record allocations in the fixed 546-record target dataset and are not confidence intervals. (B) Corresponding AUBC in gestating sows, with 95% whole-study cluster-bootstrap intervals based on 10,000 bootstrap replicates across eight independent studies. (C–F) Budget-specific R² for directly modeled FN and UN in growing pigs and gestating sows; model types are distinguished by both color and point shape. (G–H) Budget-specific R² for RN and TNE using the development-selected MLR+RF model with response-surface-guided sampling and CG-HPP updating; these endpoints were derived by nitrogen mass balance rather than independently fitted. Panels G–H use black/gray line encodings for the two physiological evaluation systems so that color is not reused for model identity. AUBC, area under the budget curve; CG-HPP, conditional Gaussian hierarchical partial pooling; FN, fecal nitrogen; MLR, multiple linear regression; RF, random forest; RN, retained nitrogen; TabM, tabular neural network; TNE, total nitrogen excretion; UN, urinary nitrogen.
